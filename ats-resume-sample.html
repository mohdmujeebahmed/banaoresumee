What is an ATS resume sample?

An ATS resume sample is a complete, filled-in example resume that shows exactly how each section should be structured and worded to pass ATS screening — not just a blank template, but a real reference with sample content.

Why ATS matters

Seeing a full sample makes abstract "ATS-friendly" rules concrete. You can see exactly how a summary, skills section, and experience bullet should actually read.

How ATS works

  1. Parsing — text extracted into structured fields
  2. Keyword Matching — content compared to job requirements
  3. Ranking — resumes scored and sorted by relevance
  4. Recruiter Search — keyword-based lookups within the ATS database

Sample breakdown, section by section

Contact Info

Full name, phone number, email, city, LinkedIn URL — placed in the main body, not a header/footer.

Professional Summary

2–3 lines summarizing role, years of experience, and top skills, written with keywords from the target job description.

Skills

A clean bullet or comma-separated list of core tools and competencies relevant to the target role.

Work Experience

Reverse-chronological, each role with 3–5 bullet points starting with action verbs and quantified outcomes.

Education

Degree, institution, graduation year, and relevant coursework or honors if applicable.

Certifications / Projects

Optional section for freshers or career changers to highlight relevant credentials or personal projects.

Best practices

  • Keep the same section order as the sample: Summary → Skills → Experience → Education
  • Use consistent date formatting throughout
  • Match keyword phrasing to the job description
  • Keep formatting minimal — no tables, columns, or graphics

Common mistakes

  • Copying the sample's content instead of using it as a structural guide
  • Skipping the summary section entirely
  • Leaving out quantified results in experience bullets
  • Ignoring keyword alignment with the specific job description
